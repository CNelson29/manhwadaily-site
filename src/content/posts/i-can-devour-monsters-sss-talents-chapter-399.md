## Alert: The Devourer Is Still Rising

If you follow system-power light novels — the kind where the protagonist starts with nothing and builds something the rulebook never accounted for — **I Can Devour Monsters' SSS Talents** by **LazyBlack** deserves a spot on your reading list. The series is ongoing and actively releasing chapters well into the 390s, making now a great time to catch up or check in on where Lian Vorhelm's journey has taken him.

## What the Novel Is About

The story takes place after the world was transformed by the arrival of interdimensional zones — called *Ruined Realms* — through which monsters began pouring into Earth. A mysterious force known as The Will responded by granting humanity talents, classes, and abilities to fight back. Almost everyone who awakened received something useful. Lian Vorhelm appeared to receive nothing at all.

That apparent emptiness turns out to be something far more disruptive: Lian can consume the monsters he defeats and absorb their talents — including those rated at the highest SSS tier. In a world built around The Will's hierarchy of power, he operates entirely outside the system's expectations. The premise works as well as it does because the gap between Lian's seeming weakness and what he actually becomes is exactly the kind of setup fans of this genre find hard to put down.

## Why It's Worth Your Time

The novel blends **Fantasy, Action, Adventure, and Romance** across its run, which gives it more range than a pure power-escalation story. Readers don't just stay for the combat — the emotional throughline keeps the investment chapter to chapter. The community has responded: the novel holds a rating of **8.2 out of 10** across reader reviews, a solid mark for an ongoing web novel competing in a crowded genre.

For fans who enjoyed *Solo Leveling* or other SSS-class fantasy series where an underestimated protagonist methodically outpaces the system that overlooked them, this one fits naturally into that category. The Ruined Realms premise also layers in a mystery the opening hook makes explicit: the world was wrong about what its greatest threat actually was. That unresolved tension gives the long-form format room to breathe.
