## When Your Second Chance Makes Everything Worse

Regression manhwa are built on a satisfying premise: go back in time, fix your mistakes, save the world. But the funniest entries in the genre take that idea and gleefully destroy it. What if you regress to stop an apocalypse that never actually happens? What if you wake up as the villain of a dating sim locked on its hardest difficulty? What if a martial god regresses — but only to level 2? These are the stories where the second chance is somehow worse than the first, and they are absolutely hilarious for it.

Every title below is a real, officially available manhwa with verified sources. No invented picks, no guesswork.

## Top 10 Funniest Regression Manhwa Ranked

### #1 — I Regressed, but the World Didn't End

Humanity's last champion burns everything — his time, his money, his soul — preparing to stop an alien apocalypse after traveling back in time. Then the apocalypse simply does not happen. No invasion, no monsters, just a mountain of debt and a job offer to help someone with game streaming. It is the most perfectly executed regression subversion in the genre: the ultra-prepared savior of civilization must now figure out how to win at content creation instead.

**Why it's great:** The gap between "apocalypse survivor" and "game streamer" is the entire joke, and the series commits to it without flinching. Watching a warrior built for extinction-level events navigate mundane problems is endlessly funny.

**Our score: 8.5/10**

### #2 — Beware the Villainess!

After an accident, a modern woman wakes up inside a clichéd romance novel as its designated villainess — and she is not remotely impressed by the story she has landed in. Rather than play her scripted role opposite a cast of insufferable love interests, she starts actively rewriting the narrative from the inside, with attitude and zero tolerance for bad storytelling. Completed, consistently hilarious, and one of the best meta-comedies the reincarnation genre has produced.

**Why it's great:** It skewers romance manhwa tropes while building genuine warmth for a heroine who approaches her ridiculous predicament with sharp practicality. The comedy has real teeth.

**Our score: 9.0/10**

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/85b51b37-0ce6-4144-a19b-6b064bc2c2ae)

### #3 — Who Made Me a Princess

The protagonist opens her eyes and discovers she has been reborn as Athanasia, a princess whose fate in the original novel is to be executed by her own father — the terrifying, cold-as-ice Emperor. Her survival strategy: become so relentlessly adorable that even a ruthless ruler cannot bring himself to go through with it. Strategic cuteness as a life-or-death mechanism turns out to be both genuinely funny and surprisingly heartwarming.

**Why it's great:** A heroine who is fully aware of the absurdity of her situation and weaponizes charm with calculated precision is a fantastic comedic engine. Light in tone but never shallow.

**Our score: 8.8/10**

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/722a45c0-5e55-40f2-929b-ff69b0989edb)

### #4 — This Villainess Wants a Divorce!

Reincarnated as Canaria, the villain of a popular novel destined to be executed by her own husband, the protagonist has one clearly defined goal: get a legal divorce before the execution happens. Not revenge, not power, not a love redemption arc — just paperwork and a quiet life somewhere far away. The single-minded practicality of that objective inside a genre that normally demands grand stakes is one of the funniest premises a manhwa has ever committed to.

**Why it's great:** Watching a "villain" try to be aggressively forgettable and unobjectionable in a world that keeps dragging her into drama is a reliably great comedic engine. Completed with a satisfying ending.

**Our score: 8.2/10**

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/7e020929-65a7-4ece-905a-7e63c52709f8)

### #5 — A Returner's Magic Should Be Special

One of the six final survivors of humanity travels back in time before the Shadow Labyrinth — the catastrophe that ended civilization — armed with full knowledge of future events and hard-won skills. The setup is classic regression power fantasy. What makes it worth watching is how relentlessly the world pushes back even when the protagonist knows exactly what is coming. Completed with a strong payoff.

**Why it's great:** The dry humor of "I know everything that will happen and things still go sideways" runs through the whole series. The comedy is lighter than most picks here, but the gap between knowledge and execution delivers consistent wit.

**Our score: 8.5/10**

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/6a468761-5bd6-4de0-a0cb-47cb456ac2e0)

### #6 — The Martial God Who Regressed Back to Level 2

A martial arts god with completely maxed-out abilities regresses — but lands at level 2, not level 1. It is a small numerical difference that throws every plan into chaos. The title alone captures the comedic energy of the series: all that accumulated power, all that knowledge of the future, and the universe still finds a way to deny him a clean reset. Available on Tapas with over 1.4 million views, which suggests the joke lands for a very large audience.

**Why it's great:** A god-tier warrior whose optimization strategies keep getting denied by stubborn reality is a premise with long comedic legs. The frustration is the fun.

**Our score: 8.0/10**

[Read on Tapas](https://tapas.io/series/the-martial-god-who-regressed-back-to-level-2)

### #7 — Villains Are Destined to Die

Penelope Eckart reincarnates as the adopted daughter of a powerful duke — and the designated villainess of a reverse harem dating sim. The immediate problem is that she loaded into the game on its hardest difficulty, where every character route leads to her death and no one in the story likes her by default. She is playing a game she never agreed to, against opponents with every structural advantage, under rules designed to ensure she loses.

**Why it's great:** The "hardest difficulty" framing is darkly funny and creates constant pressure between the comedy of her absurd situation and the genuine stakes she is navigating. Ongoing with a loyal, enthusiastic readership.

**Our score: 7.8/10**

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/14569f2f-f66a-4c67-ac7f-a37823a0fa23)

### #8 — SSS-Class Revival Hunter

Gongja Kim lives a frustrated existence in a tower full of star hunters, envying everything he cannot achieve. When he finally gains a legendary copy skill, he accidentally copies the ability of the single most powerful hunter in the tower — which triggers an involuntary time-travel regression that was never part of any plan. His situation is less "I chose to go back" and more "this happened to me and now I have to survive it."

**Why it's great:** The accidental nature of the regression gives it a distinct comedic texture. Gongja is perpetually behind the curve in a situation he did not ask for, which creates great underdog humor layered on top of the action.

**Our score: 7.5/10**

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/4a973243-952e-44d7-a50f-883b4b7c9cc2)

### #9 — I Shall Master This Family

Firentia — born of a Lombardi noble and a peasant — reincarnates into a family at the start of its long decline, after watching it fall to ruin in her previous life. Her solution is to methodically rebuild the entire dynasty from the inside, one carefully calculated social interaction at a time. The comedy comes from watching someone apply precise, chess-master logic to a family full of people who have no idea what is happening.

**Why it's great:** Firentia's unflappable rationality in the face of completely unreasonable problems is deeply satisfying. It is less laugh-out-loud funny and more "watching a genius improvise against impossible odds" — and that is its own distinct pleasure.

**Our score: 8.0/10**

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/f89ed57a-e4c0-48f5-b664-8ef88aa87fd9)

### #10 — The Skeleton Soldier Failed to Defend the Dungeon

A meek skeleton soldier swears fierce loyalty to Lady Succubus and dreams of a peaceful life together. Then they are both brutally murdered and the skeleton resets to an earlier point, forced to try again. And again. The title is not ironic — the skeleton keeps failing, keeps learning incrementally, and keeps refusing to quit despite an objectively terrible track record.

**Why it's great:** There is something absurdly funny about an undead creature that cares so much and succeeds so rarely. The dark comedic tone earns every laugh it gets, and the cumulative effect of watching the skeleton improve one painful lesson at a time is strangely compelling.

**Our score: 7.5/10**
