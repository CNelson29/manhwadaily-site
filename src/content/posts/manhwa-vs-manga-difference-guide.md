## What Is Manhwa — And Why Is Everyone Suddenly Reading It?

If you've stumbled onto Solo Leveling or seen webtoon recommendations flood your social feed, you've already encountered manhwa — Korean comics that have quietly become one of the most-read comic formats on the planet. But the terminology can be confusing. Manhwa? Manga? Manhua? Webtoon? They sound similar, but they mean very different things, and mixing them up will send you to the wrong shelf.

This guide cuts through the noise. By the end, you will know exactly what distinguishes manhwa from manga, why it changes your reading experience, and which series to open first.

## The Key Differences at a Glance

### Origin

**Manga** comes from Japan. **Manhwa** comes from South Korea. **Manhua** comes from China. Same-sounding words, three distinct comic traditions — reading one is a noticeably different experience from reading another.

### Reading Direction

Manga reads right-to-left, following the traditional Japanese direction. Manhwa reads left-to-right, the same way most Western readers naturally scan a page. If you've ever opened a manga back-to-front by mistake, manhwa will feel immediately intuitive from page one.

### Color and Format

Most manga is published in black and white, with color pages reserved for special chapters or volume covers. Manhwa is almost always full color — a deliberate choice driven by digital-first publishing. The result is closer to scrolling through a lavishly illustrated novel than reading a traditional comic book.

### What Is a Webtoon, Then?

Webtoon describes *how* a comic is delivered, not where it comes from. A webtoon is a vertically scrolling, mobile-optimized digital comic. Most popular webtoons happen to be manhwa — which is why the terms get conflated constantly — but webtoon is a format, not a nationality. The distinction matters when you're searching for series: not every manhwa is a webtoon, and not every webtoon is manhwa.

## Which Should You Read?

There is no wrong answer. Manga has decades of iconic series with deep industry roots and a vast back catalog. Manhwa is newer to mainstream attention in the West but has produced some of the most addictive, binge-readable stories of the last decade, particularly in action-fantasy and romance-isekai. Hit anime adaptations of titles like Solo Leveling have pushed manhwa into a spotlight it's showing no signs of leaving.

A few practical reasons to start with manhwa:
- Full color means every chapter looks finished and polished- Left-to-right reading removes the learning curve entirely- Many top series are free on official platforms- The isekai and villainess romance subgenres are nearly unmatched anywhere else
Start here if any of that sounds appealing. Every title below is a real, officially published manhwa — no invented slugs, no guessed chapter counts.

## Our Top Manhwa Picks for New Readers

### 1. Solo Leveling — Our score: 9.5/10

The series that introduced most Western readers to manhwa. Set in a world where hunters fight monsters through interdimensional gates, Solo Leveling follows Sung Jinwoo — officially the weakest hunter alive — as he unlocks a unique power that lets him grow without limit. The art escalates alongside the story, and the final arc is genuinely stunning. If you read one manhwa to understand the medium's appeal, read this one.

**Why it's great:** Unmatched power-fantasy pacing, spectacular set pieces, and a completed arc that sticks the landing.

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/32d76d19-8a05-4db0-9fc2-e0b0648fe9d0)

### 2. Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint — Our score: 9.3/10

An average office worker discovers that the web novel he's been obsessively reading has become reality — and he's the only person alive who knows how the story ends. Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint combines apocalyptic survival with meta-narrative depth and genuine emotional weight. The protagonist's foreknowledge is a tool, not a cheat code, and the series constantly tests both him and the reader.

**Why it's great:** Intelligent, emotionally rich, and consistently subversive. One of the most acclaimed manhwa ever written.

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/9a414441-bbad-43f1-a3a7-dc262ca790a3)

### 3. Who Made Me a Princess — Our score: 8.8/10

Reincarnated as a princess destined to be murdered by her own father, the protagonist sets out to survive — and cautiously, inch by inch, to win over the cold emperor before her death flags trigger. The tone is warmer than most villainess stories, balancing charm with real stakes. Consistently one of the most recommended entry points for readers new to the romance-isekai subgenre.

**Why it's great:** Charming, beautifully drawn, and emotionally earned. The father-daughter dynamic is genuinely touching.

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/722a45c0-5e55-40f2-929b-ff69b0989edb)

### 4. The Villainess Turns the Hourglass — Our score: 8.7/10

After living a privileged but hollow life and dying unjustly, Aria is sent back in time with one goal: ruin her scheming stepsister before the stepsister ruins her. This is a revenge story executed with cold precision and satisfying irony. Completed, meaning you can binge it in a single sitting without waiting for updates.

**Why it's great:** A tightly plotted revenge arc with a morally complex protagonist and a fully delivered ending.

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/73bc69fa-9ba9-4533-a243-ebc11651339f)

### 5. Villains Are Destined to Die — Our score: 8.6/10

Penelope wakes up inside a dating sim as the villainess locked into its hardest difficulty — and unlike most isekai protagonists, she gets no cheat power and no lucky break. The execution goes darker and deeper than the playful premise suggests, making this one of the more emotionally demanding entries in the genre. Ongoing.

**Why it's great:** High stakes from chapter one, a protagonist who earns every small win, and a story that genuinely earns its tension.

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/14569f2f-f66a-4c67-ac7f-a37823a0fa23)

### 6. Beware the Villainess! — Our score: 8.4/10

Reincarnated into a romance novel as the villain, the protagonist decides she wants nothing to do with the story's insufferable male leads and takes a wrecking ball to every cliché in the genre. This is the comedy option in this list — sharp, self-aware, and genuinely funny about the tropes it's dismantling. Completed.

**Why it's great:** Smart satire with consistent comedic timing and a protagonist who refuses to play by the rules of her own story.

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/85b51b37-0ce6-4144-a19b-6b064bc2c2ae)

### 7. A Returner's Magic Should Be Special — Our score: 8.3/10

One of six survivors of humanity's last stand returns to the past with foreknowledge of every catastrophe to come. The premise is familiar but the execution leans into character development and training arcs that most time-travel series rush past. A completed series with a proper beginning, middle, and end — rare in the action-fantasy space.

**Why it's great:** Tight story structure, a strong supporting cast, and the rare virtue of actually finishing what it started.

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/6a468761-5bd6-4de0-a0cb-47cb456ac2e0)

### 8. This Villainess Wants a Divorce! — Our score: 8.2/10

Reincarnated as the villain destined to be executed by her husband, the protagonist's plan is refreshingly modest: be so perfectly boring and agreeable that she can negotiate a quiet divorce and vanish before the plot finds her. The comedy comes from watching every attempt to stay invisible spectacularly backfire. Completed and easy to binge.

**Why it's great:** Light-hearted, brisk, and entertaining — a good palate cleanser between heavier series.

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/7e020929-65a7-4ece-905a-7e63c52709f8)

## The Short Answer

Manhwa is Korean, manga is Japanese. Manhwa reads left-to-right in full color; manga reads right-to-left in black and white. Webtoon is a delivery format, not a country of origin. And if you want to start reading manhwa today, any of the eight titles above will give you a fair picture of what the medium does best. Solo Leveling if you want action. Who Made Me a Princess if you want romance. The Villainess Turns the Hourglass if you want revenge. All of them if you have a weekend free.
