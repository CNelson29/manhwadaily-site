## Why Completed Matters More Than You Think

There is a specific kind of reader frustration unique to manhwa: you invest thirty, sixty, a hundred chapters in a system-fantasy thriller, then the series goes on indefinite hiatus or ends without resolution. Solo Leveling is the rare exception — a full story with a satisfying conclusion. Finding completed manhwa with that same DNA is genuinely hard, which is why this list exists.

The titles below are ranked by how well they replicate the experience: a threatening world the protagonist must out-think and out-level, genuine tension in every arc, and — where possible — a real ending. Series that are still ongoing are included where they are exceptional enough to earn a place, but they are flagged clearly so you know what you are signing up for.

## Rankings: Best Completed Thriller Manhwa Like Solo Leveling

### 1. Solo Leveling

**Our score: 9.5 / 10**  |  **Status: Completed**

The benchmark against which every other hunter-genre manhwa is measured. Sung Jinwoo begins as the weakest hunter alive and systematically dismantles every power structure above him. The thriller element is real — early arcs carry genuine dread, and the pacing knows exactly when to slow down and when to detonate. The ending delivers.

**Why it is great:** The art escalates in ambition alongside the protagonist's power. Few manhwa make a power fantasy feel earned rather than handed over, and Solo Leveling does it consistently across its entire run. If you have not read it, start here.

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/32d76d19-8a05-4db0-9fc2-e0b0648fe9d0)

### 2. Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint

**Our score: 9.2 / 10**  |  **Status: Ongoing**

Dokja Kim is the only person who has read the web novel that just became reality, which means he knows how the story ends — and more importantly, how everyone he cares about dies. The psychological edge here is sharper than Solo Leveling: Dokja's advantage is knowledge, not raw power, and watching him use it is the closest thing manhwa has to a chess thriller.

**Why it is great:** The emotional stakes are built carefully over hundreds of chapters. The cast earns its place in a way that most action manhwa never manage. Still ongoing, but already one of the best things in the genre.

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/9a414441-bbad-43f1-a3a7-dc262ca790a3)

### 3. SSS-Class Revival Hunter

**Our score: 8.9 / 10**  |  **Status: Ongoing**

Gongja Kim copies the ability of the S-Class hunter who kills him — and wakes up, resurrected, able to use it. The setup is a dark inversion of the usual power fantasy: the protagonist is defined by his death, and every revival sharpens both his abilities and his psychology. The Tower setting allows for the same escalating stakes that made Solo Leveling addictive.

**Why it is great:** The tension between Gongja's growing power and his moral deterioration is handled with more nuance than most genre peers. Ongoing, but the arc structure keeps each section satisfying on its own terms.

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/4a973243-952e-44d7-a50f-883b4b7c9cc2)

### 4. A Returner's Magic Should Be Special

**Our score: 8.7 / 10**  |  **Status: Completed**

Desir Arman is one of six survivors of humanity's deadliest catastrophe and travels back in time to prevent it. Unlike most time-travel action manhwa, the threat here is specific and well-designed — the Shadow Labyrinth is a concrete antagonist, not a vague darkness — and Desir's approach to stopping it requires tactical intelligence alongside escalating power.

**Why it is great:** A completed run with a genuine ending is rare in this genre. The time-travel mechanic adds thriller tension without becoming a crutch, and the tactical combat keeps every major battle interesting. A full satisfying read from start to finish.

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/6a468761-5bd6-4de0-a0cb-47cb456ac2e0)

### 5. Second Life Ranker

**Our score: 8.4 / 10**  |  **Status: Hiatus**

Yeonwoo learns that his brother was betrayed and killed inside the Tower — then uses his brother's hidden records to climb it himself, targeting everyone responsible. The revenge thriller framing is tighter than most, and the world-building around the Tower's mechanics is unusually detailed for the genre.

**Why it is great:** The emotional engine — grief transformed into methodical revenge — gives the power escalation genuine weight. Currently on hiatus, worth knowing before you start, but the content that exists is high quality and worth the investment.

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/1ffca916-3ad7-46d2-9591-a9b39e639971)

### 6. Kill the Hero

**Our score: 8.2 / 10**  |  **Status: Completed**

The protagonist was betrayed and murdered by the guild leader he trusted — and wakes up years earlier with full memory of what is coming. His goal is not heroism: it is to survive long enough to dismantle the man who killed him. The dark revenge premise is executed with enough restraint to keep the thriller tension alive across the full run.

**Why it is great:** One of the most satisfying complete reads in the hunter genre. The antagonist is a credible, calculating villain rather than a cartoon, which raises every confrontation considerably. Completed at around 153 chapters per community tracking.

[Search on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=Kill+the+Hero)

### 7. The Skeleton Soldier Failed to Defend the Dungeon

**Our score: 8.0 / 10**  |  **Status: Ongoing**

A skeleton soldier watches his master die repeatedly and resets — accumulating memory, grief, and eventually a purpose beyond mere loyalty. The time-loop mechanic turns what could be a straightforward action series into something with genuine psychological weight. Each loop strips away more of what the protagonist thought he understood about his world.

**Why it is great:** The horror-adjacent atmosphere separates it from most action manhwa. Ongoing, but the episodic loop structure means any stopping point is reasonably satisfying. A strong pick for readers who want their thriller grounded in atmosphere rather than pure spectacle.

[Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/d993f789-e7e5-4832-92fd-37614220b427)

### 8. I Am the Sorcerer King

**Our score: 7.9 / 10**  |  **Status: Completed**

Published during the same era as Solo Leveling and sharing its Gate-and-hunters premise, I Am the Sorcerer King follows a man who awakens as the reincarnation of a powerful sorcerer. The setup is familiar but the magic system is more elaborate than most of its contemporaries, giving the action sequences a tactical layer that thriller readers will appreciate.

**Why it is great:** Completed, which is increasingly rare in the hunter genre. A solid full read for anyone who exhausted Solo Leveling and wants something in the same world-design tradition without starting an ongoing series.

[Search on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=I+Am+the+Sorcerer+King)

### 9. Killing Killer

**Our score: 7.6 / 10**  |  **Status: Completed**

Si-U Lee spent a decade hunting the man who murdered his parents. When he finally kills him and learns the truth — the real killer is still out there — the story shifts from a revenge thriller into something more morally complex. Completed at 184 chapters, it delivers a full investigation arc with a real resolution.

**Why it is great:** The thriller mechanics here are closer to crime fiction than system fantasy, making it a strong entry point for readers who find the RPG-progression genre too formulaic. Completed on Naver, with the entire story accessible.

[Search on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=Killing+Killer)

## How to Choose Your Next Read

If you want a guaranteed full story with a real ending: start with **Solo Leveling**, then **A Returner's Magic Should Be Special**, then **Kill the Hero**. All three are completed and deliver satisfying conclusions.

If you are willing to follow ongoing series for quality: **Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint** is the strongest manhwa in this genre currently publishing, and **SSS-Class Revival Hunter** is close behind.

If you want pure thriller with less system fantasy: **Killing Killer** is the furthest from the Solo Leveling template and the most satisfying as a crime thriller in its own right.
