## Why Villain Protagonists Are Taking Over Manhwa

Forget the noble hero saving the world out of pure-hearted goodness — the most electrifying manhwas of the past decade have handed the spotlight to the **villain**. These are protagonists who lie, manipulate, crush their enemies without mercy, and somehow make you root for every second of it. Whether they're reincarnated into the body of a story's big bad, or simply chose the dark path from the start, villain protagonists deliver a reading experience that's raw, unpredictable, and utterly addictive.

On **manhwadaily.com**, we've combed through hundreds of titles to bring you the definitive list of the **10 Best Villain Protagonist Manhwas You Need to Read** right now. Each entry below is ranked for its storytelling depth, art quality, character complexity, and that special thrill of watching someone morally questionable absolutely dominate. Buckle up — things are about to get deliciously dark.

## Rankings: 10 Best Villain Protagonist Manhwas

### 1. 🥇 The Villain Wants to Live (악당은 살고 싶다)

**Rating: 9.6/10**

Deluard, the empire's most feared villain, suddenly gains the memories of a reader who knows he's destined to die at the story's end. Armed with foreknowledge and zero desire to meet his scripted fate, he begins rewriting his own destiny with cunning, charm, and calculated ruthlessness. What makes this manhwa extraordinary is how it balances dark humor with genuine emotional stakes — Deluard is terrifying, hilarious, and oddly sympathetic all at once.

**Why It's Great:** The meta-narrative of a villain who knows he's a villain is executed brilliantly. The art is expressive and dynamic, and the political scheming rivals anything in the genre. Every chapter leaves you desperate for the next.

### 2. 🥈 I'm the Villain, So I'll Just Enjoy Myself (나는 악당이니까 즐기겠다)

**Rating: 9.3/10**

A modern-day office worker reincarnates as the primary antagonist of a popular fantasy novel and decides — rather than fight his fate — to simply enjoy the perks of being powerful and feared. His laid-back attitude toward villainy creates hilarious and surprisingly touching moments as the original story's plot crumbles around him. The contrast between his casual demeanor and the chaos he effortlessly causes is pure comedic gold wrapped in serious action.

**Why It's Great:** The protagonist's refreshing indifference to heroic conventions makes every interaction unpredictable. The world-building is rich, the side characters are memorable, and the action sequences are genuinely jaw-dropping.

### 3. 🥉 Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint (전지적 독자 시점)

**Rating: 9.5/10**

Kim Dokja is the sole reader of an obscure web novel — until the world transforms into the apocalyptic scenario described in its pages. Using his complete knowledge of the story's events, he navigates a world of monsters, constellations, and impossible scenarios, often making morally grey choices that blur the line between hero and villain. This is one of the most ambitious manhwa adaptations ever produced, with a scope that rivals epic fantasy novels.

**Why It's Great:** The meta-fictional layers are mind-bending, the emotional payoffs are devastating, and Dokja's willingness to sacrifice and manipulate makes him one of manhwa's most complex protagonists. The art team brings every apocalyptic set piece to life magnificently.

### 4. The Descent of the Demonic Master (마교의 주인이 되었다)

**Rating: 9.1/10**

After dying twice — once in a tragic accident and once in a brutal cultivation world — Jinho Kang reincarnates back into modern Korea carrying the terrifying power and ruthless mindset of a demonic martial arts master. He has no patience for weakness, no tolerance for injustice, and absolutely no hesitation to destroy anyone who threatens those he cares about. The collision of demonic cultivation logic with everyday Korean life creates an endlessly entertaining dynamic.

**Why It's Great:** The action choreography is some of the best in manhwa — fast, brutal, and beautifully illustrated. Jinho's cold pragmatism makes every confrontation feel genuinely dangerous, and his slow emotional thawing is handled with surprising nuance.

### 5. Reincarnation of the Suicidal Battle God (자살 전쟁신의 환생)

**Rating: 8.9/10**

Zephyr, humanity's last warrior, dies in a final desperate battle against the demon lord — only to wake up ten years in the past with all his power and trauma intact. Rather than playing the noble hero, he pursues revenge and survival with a ferocity that borders on terrifying, eliminating threats before they can grow and trusting almost no one. His methods are extreme, his resolve is absolute, and watching him dismantle the forces that destroyed his world is deeply satisfying.

**Why It's Great:** The time-regression premise is executed with unusual emotional weight — Zephyr isn't just overpowered, he's broken, and that psychological depth elevates every battle. The demon designs and combat art are spectacular.

### 6. The Novel's Extra (소설 속 엑스트라)

**Rating: 8.8/10**

A writer wakes up inside his own unfinished novel as a minor villain character named Kim Hajin, armed with the ability to write new items and skills into existence through a mysterious status window. Knowing the story's plot but unable to fully control it, he must navigate a world of heroes, monsters, and political intrigue while trying not to get killed by the very narrative he created. The self-aware storytelling and creative power system make this a standout in the isekai-villain subgenre.

**Why It's Great:** The author-inside-his-own-story concept is brilliantly exploited for both comedy and tension. Hajin's creative problem-solving and gradual shift from background character to central force is one of manhwa's most satisfying character arcs.

### 7. Trash of the Count's Family (백작가의 망나니가 되었다)

**Rating: 9.0/10**

An ordinary reader gets transported into a novel as Cale Henituse, a minor noble known for being a useless, wine-guzzling trash — and he intends to keep that reputation while secretly accumulating power and avoiding the dangerous main plot entirely. His plan to live a peaceful, lazy life keeps getting derailed by his own accidental heroism and the loyal companions who refuse to leave his side. The gap between Cale's self-image as a scheming coward and his actual impact on the world is endlessly hilarious and heartwarming.

**Why It's Great:** The ensemble cast is one of manhwa's best, and the slow-burn world-building rewards patient readers enormously. Cale's brand of reluctant villainy — always claiming selfish motives while doing genuinely good things — is uniquely charming.

### 8. Villain to Kill (빌런을 죽이는 빌런)

**Rating: 8.7/10**

Cassian, a powerful Psyker hero, has his body stolen and wakes up in the body of a notorious villain — and rather than trying to reclaim his old life, he decides to use his new villainous identity to hunt down and destroy other villains from the inside. The superhero-meets-dark-antihero premise is executed with stylish flair, and the moral ambiguity of fighting evil through evil means is explored with genuine intelligence. Every arc raises the stakes in unexpected ways.

**Why It's Great:** The superpower system is creative and visually stunning in action. Cassian's dual identity creates constant dramatic tension, and the series never lets you get too comfortable with who the real villains are.

### 9. The Dark Magician Transmigrates After 66666 Years (66666년 만에 환생한 흑마법사)

**Rating: 8.6/10**

Diablo Volfir, the most powerful dark magician in history, was sealed away by the gods for 66,666 years and reincarnates as a young boy named Jamie Welton — with all his memories, hatred, and ambition fully intact. His singular goal is revenge against the gods who imprisoned him, and he pursues it with cold, methodical fury even as a child in a new body. The contrast between his adorable appearance and his ancient, terrifying mind is both funny and genuinely unsettling.

**Why It's Great:** The god-slaying ambition gives the series an epic scope that few manhwas match. Jamie's gradual power recovery is deeply satisfying, and the dark magic system is one of the most visually inventive in the genre.

### 10. Eleceed (일렉시드)

**Rating: 8.5/10**

While not a traditional villain protagonist, Jiwoo Seo — a kind-hearted boy with lightning-fast reflexes — partners with Kayden, a ruthless, calculating awakener trapped in a cat's body whose methods are decidedly more villainous than heroic. Kayden's cold pragmatism and willingness to use any means necessary to achieve his goals makes him the true villain-protagonist heart of the series. Together they navigate a hidden world of awakeners, conspiracies, and escalating threats with explosive action and sharp humor.

**Why It's Great:** The art by the creator of Noblesse is absolutely gorgeous, with some of the cleanest action panels in manhwa. Kayden's character development from ruthless manipulator to reluctant mentor is one of the genre's most rewarding arcs.

## Final Thoughts: Embrace the Dark Side of Manhwa

The villain protagonist genre represents manhwa storytelling at its most daring and psychologically rich. These aren't simple power fantasies — they're explorations of morality, identity, and what it truly means to be good or evil when the world refuses to play by fair rules. Every title on this list offers something unique: whether it's the meta-fictional brilliance of *Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint*, the darkly comedic charm of *Trash of the Count's Family*, or the cold, methodical fury of *The Dark Magician Transmigrates After 66666 Years*.
