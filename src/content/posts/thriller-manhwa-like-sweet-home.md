## Why Sweet Home Still Sets the Standard

Sweet Home changed what Korean horror comics could be. Cha Hyun-soo moves into a rundown apartment complex and watches his neighbors transform into monsters shaped by their deepest desires — the show-off becomes a muscle beast, the glutton becomes a writhing mass of hunger. The creature designs are unforgettable, but what makes Sweet Home stick is the psychological core: survival not just against external monsters, but against your own worst impulses.

Finding manhwa that hit the same nerve requires looking beyond the creature designs. Below are ten verified, officially published series that share Sweet Home's mix of creature horror, confined-space survival, and psychological weight — confirmed against multiple sources before inclusion.

## 10 Survival Horror Manhwa to Read After Sweet Home

### 1. Bastard — Our Score: 9.5/10

Jin is a meek high school student living in constant fear. His father is a beloved neurosurgeon by day and a serial killer who uses Jin as bait by night. What makes Bastard devastating is not the violence but the slow dissolution of Jin's ability to distinguish right from wrong when survival depends on complicity with something monstrous.

**Why it belongs here:** Created by the same team behind Sweet Home — Carnby Kim and Youngchan Hwang — Bastard channels the same inward-turning horror and the same refusal to let its protagonist be simply good. At over 151.6 million views on Webtoon, it is Sweet Home's closest companion piece and the first series any fan of the latter should read.

[Read Bastard on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=Bastard)

### 2. All of Us Are Dead — Our Score: 9.0/10

A zombie virus breaks out inside a Korean high school, trapping students in the building as the outbreak spreads through the city. Infection cascades through classrooms in minutes, forcing students into survival alliances where trust is a liability and every hallway becomes a decision point with permanent consequences.

**Why it belongs here:** The confined-space survival dynamic mirrors Sweet Home's apartment-complex premise almost directly, with a school substituting for the building. The series layers teenage social hierarchies onto every survival calculation, making character decisions feel doubly charged.

[Read All of Us Are Dead on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=All+of+Us+Are+Dead)

### 3. Hell Is Other People — Our Score: 8.8/10

Jongu Yun is a young man in his twenties who moves to Seoul and rents the cheapest room he can find in a run-down boarding house. The residents are strange, the building is dilapidated, and Jongu stays because he cannot afford to leave — until mysterious events make him question whether his neighbors are something other than human. The series has accumulated 9.4 million views on Webtoon.

**Why it belongs here:** The premise of being trapped with something inhuman in a confined residential space is almost identical to Sweet Home, shifted from an apartment complex to a boarding house. Where Sweet Home externalizes horror into striking creature designs, Hell Is Other People keeps the threat ambiguous and psychological for far longer.

[Read Hell Is Other People on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/thriller/hell-is-other-people/list?title_no=3176)

### 4. Duty After School — Our Score: 8.5/10

Alien spheres appear across Korea without warning, releasing creatures that attack on sight. High school students — weeks from graduation — are conscripted into military units and sent to fight. The series is unflinching: characters built carefully over multiple chapters are lost without ceremony, and survivors cope in ways that feel realistic rather than heroic.

**Why it belongs here:** The horror here comes not from creatures alone but from the institutional machinery sending teenagers into a situation they cannot possibly be prepared for. That same gap between civilian vulnerability and overwhelming monstrous threat drives Sweet Home's most effective sequences.

[Read Duty After School on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=Duty+After+School)

### 5. One Day, Suddenly, Seoul Is — Our Score: 8.3/10

A mysterious virus spreads through Seoul, infecting people through mutant cells that multiply and transform victims into aggressive creatures. The city descends into chaos as neighborhoods stop feeling safe within hours of the first outbreak. Completed at 181 chapters, the series traces the full arc of a city-scale collapse from initial infection to aftermath.

**Why it belongs here:** The scale is larger than Sweet Home — a full city versus a single apartment building — but the core question is identical: how do ordinary people survive when the threat is biological, external, and spreading through the population immediately around them?

[Read One Day, Suddenly, Seoul Is on MangaBaka](https://mangabaka.org/6366)

### 6. Tales of the Unusual — Our Score: 7.9/10

An anthology horror series from Webtoon, created by Seongdae Oh, collecting standalone stories built around dark moral twists — folk horror, urban legend riffs, and supernatural morality plays that rarely end the way you expect. With 164.3 million views, it is one of the most-read horror anthologies on the platform.

**Why it belongs here:** If what drew you to Sweet Home was its monsters-as-metaphor dimension rather than the survival action, Tales of the Unusual delivers that reading in concentrated form, story by story. Each episode is self-contained, meaning you can start anywhere and the series never loses momentum.

[Read Tales of the Unusual on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=Tales+of+the+Unusual)

### 7. I'm the Grim Reaper — Our Score: 7.7/10

A young woman is sent to Hell and offered a deal: work as a Grim Reaper collecting sinners' souls on Earth, or face eternal punishment. The series mixes supernatural thriller elements with moral dilemmas about guilt, judgment, and what actually qualifies someone as deserving death. With 14.8 million views on Webtoon, it has found a large and committed readership.

**Why it belongs here:** The supernatural horror and moral complexity echo Sweet Home's central meditation on what separates humans from monsters. The survival element here is existential — the protagonist fights to preserve her humanity while performing a function that requires abandoning it.

[Read I'm the Grim Reaper on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=I%27m+the+Grim+Reaper)

### 8. Unholy Blood — Our Score: 7.5/10

A half-vampire protagonist has spent years hiding her nature, living as a human while suppressing what she is. When escalating vampire violence makes concealment impossible, she is forced into the open — and forced to confront whether fighting monsters requires becoming one in the process.

**Why it belongs here:** The protagonist's dual nature directly mirrors Cha Hyun-soo's transformation arc in Sweet Home. The tension between humanity and monstrosity is not a backdrop here — it is the engine of the horror, which is exactly how Sweet Home uses its own transformation premise.

[Read Unholy Blood on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=Unholy+Blood)

### 9. Clinic of Horrors — Our Score: 7.4/10

A horror series from Webtoon with 11.9 million reads that has built its audience through consistent tone and scenarios that take familiar, supposedly safe spaces and make them threatening. The title signals the approach: a place associated with care and healing becomes something else entirely.

**Why it belongs here:** Sweet Home's most effective sequences use domestic spaces — apartment hallways, stairwells, shared kitchens — as the terrain of horror. Clinic of Horrors applies the same spatial inversion to a different kind of familiar space, with comparable unsettling results.

[Read Clinic of Horrors on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=Clinic+of+Horrors)

### 10. Hand Jumper — Our Score: 7.2/10

A psychological thriller from Webtoon that has accumulated 6.5 million reads through sustained tension and long-strip pacing. Hand Jumper operates at a different register than creature horror — the threats here are rooted in human behavior — but the series rewards the same kind of forward-momentum reading that makes survival horror hard to put down.

**Why it belongs here:** For readers who found Sweet Home's psychological undertow as compelling as its monster designs, Hand Jumper is a natural follow-up when you want the tension calibrated down from body horror toward psychological dread. A focused thriller with a committed fanbase.

[Read Hand Jumper on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=Hand+Jumper)
