## Why Completed Comedy Manhwa Are the Best Weekend Read

There is something uniquely freeing about picking up a comedy manhwa that is already finished. No mid-season hiatuses, no cliffhangers dangling for months, no doomscrolling for release dates. You open it on a Friday night and by Sunday you have laughed your way through a complete story with a real ending. The picks below are confirmed completed series — every arc resolved, every comedic payoff delivered.

Comedy in manhwa covers more tonal ground than most lists acknowledge. The eight series here span genre-aware villainess parody, absurdist isekai premise comedy, dry slice-of-life humor, and dark revenge satisfaction. Something on this list will match your specific flavor of funny — and all of it is available to binge right now.

## 8 Best Completed Comedy Manhwa Ranked

### 1. Beware the Villainess! — Our Score: 9.3/10

After an accident lands a modern woman inside a clichéd romance novel as its designated villain, she immediately starts editing the plot. The angelic heroine gets critiqued, the devoted suitors get called out for their nonsense, and the protagonist rewrites the entire romantic script on her own terms. Every chapter fires a comedic beat, the self-awareness is surgical, and the completed run means every joke builds toward a genuinely satisfying finish.

**Why it's great:** This is the gold standard for genre-parody comedy in manhwa. It rewards readers who know isekai tropes but works just as well if you don't. Pure comedy engine from first page to last.

Normal Duke ran to 100 episodes on WEBTOON before wrapping with a confirmed series finale in August 2026, delivering consistent dry humor rooted in one man's losing battle against greatness and absurdity. The comedic timing is excellent, the central joke never gets old, and the complete episode count makes it a perfect weekend binge.

**Why it's great:** A rare WEBTOON comedy with a real ending that pays off the premise. 100 episodes of escalating absurdity resolved properly — exactly what a binge-weekend list needs.

### 3. The Greatest Estate Developer — Our Score: 8.8/10

A civil engineering student wakes up inside a doomed fantasy novel and concludes that his best survival strategy is infrastructure. Foundation work, drainage systems, and construction logistics have no business being this funny — and yet this completed manhwa wrings extraordinary comedy out of a practical modern mind meeting a world that expects dramatic swordfights and magic. The genre mismatch is the entire joke, and it never stops working from first chapter to finale.

**Why it's great:** A wholly original comedic premise executed with total commitment. Once the binge starts, stopping becomes genuinely difficult.

### 4. This Villainess Wants a Divorce! — Our Score: 8.5/10

Reincarnated as Canaria, the villain of a clichéd romance novel destined to be executed by her own husband, the protagonist has exactly one objective: file for divorce before the execution scene and escape with her life. The comedy comes from the meticulous social maneuvering required to dissolve a royal marriage without anyone understanding why, played against a cast that keeps misreading every move. Completed, tight, no filler — just scheming-for-survival humor all the way through.

**Why it's great:** A perfectly contained comedic premise with a clear finish line. The deadpan survival instinct of the lead is the funniest thing in the series.

### 5. A Returner's Magic Should Be Special — Our Score: 8.3/10

Desir Arman, one of six survivors of the apocalyptic Shadow Labyrinth, wakes up ten years in the past inside a magic academy with complete knowledge of how events will unfold. The series wears action and drama on the surface, but its Comedy tag is genuinely earned through the gap between what Desir knows and what every earnest, unsuspecting person around him believes is happening. Completed, so every arc payoff is already waiting for you.

**Why it's great:** The comedy operates on dramatic irony — watching Desir calmly solve crises that terrify everyone else is its own consistently rewarding form of humor.

### 6. True Beauty — Our Score: 8.1/10

Lim Jugyeong discovers the transformative power of makeup and navigates high school with two completely separate identities — the bare-faced self she hides from everyone and the made-up persona the whole school adores. What could have been pure drama runs primarily on comedy: the escalating, increasingly chaotic work required to keep a secret in a school full of people who keep almost finding out. Completed on WEBTOON with a full run that rewards bingeing for the density of its comedic setups alone.

**Why it's great:** Accessible, high-energy comedy built on a sharp social premise. The humor around the double-identity maintenance is the engine the whole series runs on.

The dramatic framework is real, but what the story delivers is dark comedy: Aria systematically out-maneuvers someone who considers herself a masterful manipulator, with such pettiness and precision that completed readers describe the experience as deeply, uncomfortably funny. Every scheme landing exactly as planned is its own genre of satisfaction.

**Why it's great:** Revenge comedy with a completed arc. The humor is darker than anything else on this list, but the payoff is proportionally more satisfying.

## How to Choose Your Weekend Binge

For pure, uncut comedy: start with **Beware the Villainess!** or **Normal Duke** — both are engineered for laughs first, everything else second. If you prefer humor delivered through an isekai or action framework, **The Greatest Estate Developer** and **A Returner's Magic Should Be Special** use genre subversion rather than gag setups. For darker comedic satisfaction, **The Villainess Turns the Hourglass** rewards patience with a revenge arc you can watch snap shut in a single sitting. All eight are done. No waiting required.
