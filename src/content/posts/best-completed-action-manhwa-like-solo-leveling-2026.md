## The Honest Case for Completed Manhwa Like Solo Leveling

Most lists on this topic are quietly misleading. They recommend series that are still publishing, on seasonal hiatus, or barely past chapter 50. [Chapterbrief flagged this in May 2026](https://www.chapterbrief.net/blog/completed-manhwa-like-solo-leveling): even Second Life Ranker — a fixture on every “completed” recommendation list — returned for Season 4 in April 2026 and is still active. We label every entry honestly below. If you came strictly for finished stories, Solo Leveling and A Returner's Magic Should Be Special are your two genuine completed action picks. The rest are the best ongoing series in the same mold — worth starting now.

## Rankings

### #1 — Solo Leveling

**Status: Completed** | **Our Score: 9.8/10**

The series that redefined what manhwa could be. Sung Jin-Woo begins as the weakest hunter alive and rises through a private leveling system invisible to everyone else, eventually becoming the Shadow Monarch. The art is consistently cinematic — fluid action, dramatic lighting, and panel composition that few manhwa have matched. [Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/32d76d19-8a05-4db0-9fc2-e0b0648fe9d0) | [Search on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=Solo+Leveling)

**Why it's great:** It finishes, and the ending is earned. Every manhwa on this list exists because Solo Leveling proved the genre had a ceiling worth chasing.

### #2 — Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint

**Status: Ongoing** | **Our Score: 9.6/10**

Kim Dokja spent years as the sole reader of a niche web novel called *Three Ways to Survive the Apocalypse* — and when the novel's scenario becomes real, he is the only person alive who knows how the story ends. He uses that knowledge as his weapon in a world collapsing around everyone else. The emotional stakes go significantly deeper than typical system manhwa. [Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/9a414441-bbad-43f1-a3a7-dc262ca790a3)

**Why it's great:** Combines Solo Leveling's system mechanics with genuine character writing. The action sequences are earned, not handed over. Ongoing, but the quality justifies the investment.

### #3 — SSS-Class Revival Hunter

**Status: Ongoing** | **Our Score: 9.4/10**

Gongja Kim lives in a tower-dungeon world where elite hunters are gods and he is invisible. One day he copies a legendary skill from the most dangerous hunter in the tower — and is immediately killed for it. That copied skill rewinds time after each death, letting him keep every power he has ever stolen. The revenge arc begins. [Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/4a973243-952e-44d7-a50f-883b4b7c9cc2)

**Why it's great:** The closest spiritual successor to Solo Leveling currently running. Zero-to-god progression, a structured tower dungeon, and a protagonist whose growth feels genuinely satisfying rather than arbitrarily granted.

### #4 — A Returner's Magic Should Be Special

**Status: Completed** | **Our Score: 8.7/10**

Desir Arman survives humanity's final catastrophe — the Shadow Labyrinth — as one of only six remaining humans alive. Sent back in time with full knowledge of what is coming, he enters a magic academy and begins dismantling the future disaster before it starts. The time-travel framing gives the power fantasy a strategic layer most action manhwa skip entirely. [Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/6a468761-5bd6-4de0-a0cb-47cb456ac2e0)

**Why it's great:** One of the rare completed action manhwa that actually sticks its landing. If you want a full story with a proper beginning, middle, and end — this is your answer alongside Solo Leveling itself.

### #5 — Second Life Ranker

**Status: Hiatus / Seasonal (active as of 2026)** | **Our Score: 8.5/10**

Yeonwoo's twin brother vanished inside a mysterious tower and was later killed by betrayal. When Yeonwoo receives his brother's hidden pocket watch, he learns the full truth — and enters the same tower to settle the score. The structured tower-dungeon progression and revenge-driven narrative will feel immediately familiar to Solo Leveling readers. [Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/1ffca916-3ad7-46d2-9591-a9b39e639971)

**Why it's great:** Strong dungeon mythology and a protagonist whose motivation stays clear across a long run. Be aware: this series is not completed and has run in seasonal installments — approach it as an ongoing investment.

### #6 — The Skeleton Soldier Failed to Defend the Dungeon

**Status: Ongoing** | **Our Score: 8.3/10**

A skeleton soldier dies protecting its master and wakes up back at the beginning of its existence with all memories intact. What follows is a dark, emotionally layered time-loop story about an undead creature developing something resembling agency and purpose. The dungeon survival premise conceals a much more philosophical narrative underneath. [Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/d993f789-e7e5-4832-92fd-37614220b427)

**Why it's great:** Subverts the power-fantasy formula entirely — you are rooting for a monster, not a chosen hero. Each reset carries genuine cost, which creates stakes the genre rarely achieves.

### #7 — The Max Level Hero Strikes Back

**Status: Ongoing** | **Our Score: 8.1/10**

Powerless Prince Davey falls into a coma; his soul travels to a hall where the greatest heroes of history train him across what feels like a thousand years. He wakes up with absurd hidden power and decides to keep playing the meek weakling — for now. The comedic timing on the gap between his reputation and reality is consistently sharp. [Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/b407de00-75a5-415a-a001-585fb41b9cf2)

**Why it's great:** The OP-protagonist-hiding-his-strength trope executed with actual payoffs. If Solo Leveling's early weakest-hunter arc was your favorite stretch, this series lives in that space throughout.

### #8 — Beware the Villainess!

**Status: Completed** | **Our Score: 7.9/10**

A modern woman wakes up as the villain of a clichéd romance novel and immediately decides to break every rule of the genre — loudly, unapologetically, and often with physical force. The action is comedic rather than epic, but the protagonist's aggressive refusal to accept the role she was handed carries the same energy that makes Solo Leveling's early chapters satisfying. [Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/85b51b37-0ce6-4144-a19b-6b064bc2c2ae)

**Why it's great:** Completed from start to finish, genuinely funny, and tonally distinct enough to feel like a palette cleanser. The best pick on this list if you want something lighter that still delivers a protagonist who refuses to stay weak.
