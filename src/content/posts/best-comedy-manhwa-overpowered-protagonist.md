## Why Comedy and Overpowered MCs Are a Perfect Match

The best comedy manhwa featuring overpowered protagonists aren't straightforward power fantasies — they weaponize the OP premise for laughs. Whether it's a hero desperately trying to hide godlike strength while one-shotting every boss he touches, or a max-level prince returning to humble the nobles who dismissed him, the comedy hits hardest when the power gap is at its widest. These eight picks nail that balance, ranging from absurdist WEBTOON gems to binge-worthy Tapas series.

## Rankings

### 1. +99 Reinforced Wooden Stick

What happens when you max out the most common item in the game? Absolute, hilarious chaos. This WEBTOON comedy — now deep into its second season — takes the concept of a +99 reinforced wooden stick and wrings every drop of absurdist humor from it. The premise is deceptively simple: an ordinary object upgraded to its absolute limit becomes a weapon of mass destruction in the hands of someone who should not have that much power. By Season 2, the series is pitting that stick against Cthulhu-level threats.

**Why it's great:** The comedy escalates brilliantly arc after arc. The gap between the weapon's humble origins and its terrifying power level is the engine that keeps every chapter funny, and the series has the longevity to prove the formula doesn't wear out.

**Our score: 9.0/10** — The gold standard for absurdist OP comedy on WEBTOON.

[Read on Webtoon →](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=%2B99+Reinforced+Wooden+Stick)

### 2. I Was More Overpowered Than the Hero, So I Hid My Power!

Reincarnated into a fantasy world with the lifelong dream of being the cool, unassuming hero who secretly holds earth-shattering power, this protagonist finally gets everything he wanted — and then immediately discovers that hiding it is completely impossible. Every monster, every dungeon boss, every rival falls in a single hit no matter how desperately he tries to hold back. The premise captures the 'hide your power level' trope and then systematically dismantles it for laughs, chapter by chapter.

**Why it's great:** The comedy comes from the protagonist's absolute, earnest commitment to the low-profile bit despite constant, spectacular failure. A reader score of 8.7 on PanelVerse reflects how well it sustains a premise that sounds like it should get old fast — it doesn't.

**Our score: 8.8/10** — A love letter to the hidden-power trope that deconstructs it hilariously.

[Read on Webtoon →](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=I+Was+More+Overpowered+Than+the+Hero)

### 3. The Max Level Hero Strikes Back

Prince Davey was considered powerless and worthless by everyone around him — until an arrow struck him down and his soul traveled to the Hall of Heroes, where the greatest warriors in history trained him personally. When he wakes up, he carries the knowledge and might of a thousand legendary heroes compressed into one body. The nobles who dismissed him are about to have a very bad time.

**Why it's great:** The 'revenge of the underestimated' setup is a classic, but this series earns its comedy from how thoroughly and consistently Davey dismantles every expectation. The scale of the power gap keeps the payoffs landing, and there's genuine comedic timing in how he chooses to reveal — or not reveal — just how far beyond everyone else he now is.

**Our score: 8.7/10** — Supremely satisfying OP fantasy with real comedic timing.

[Read on MangaDex →](https://mangadex.org/title/b407de00-75a5-415a-a001-585fb41b9cf2)

### 4. The Extra Is Too Powerful

In most manhwa, the 'extra' — a background character with no narrative significance — exists to get defeated or ignored. Not here. This Tapas series flips the formula entirely: the extra is somehow the most powerful person in every room he enters, and neither he nor anyone else quite knows what to do about it. The comedic tension between his irrelevant status and his ability to wreck everything around him is the whole show.

**Why it's great:** Over 645,000 views on Tapas confirm this premise connects strongly with readers. It's a meta-aware take on manhwa tropes that rewards genre veterans while staying accessible to newcomers — the best kind of inside joke that doesn't require being in the club to enjoy.

**Our score: 8.5/10** — Smart genre comedy with satisfying OP moments throughout.

[Read on Tapas →](https://tapas.io/series/the-extra-is-too-powerful/info)

### 5. A Returner's Magic Should Be Special

Desir Arman is one of only six survivors of the Shadow Labyrinth — humanity's deadliest catastrophe. When he gets a second chance and travels back in time to before it all began, he carries knowledge that makes him functionally untouchable in a world that has no idea what's coming. The comedy is bone-dry: Desir is always several steps ahead, and everyone around him is perpetually, helplessly catching up.

**Why it's great:** Unlike series that lean purely on raw power, Desir wins through preparation and foreknowledge, which makes OP moments feel earned rather than arbitrary. The completed status means you can binge the full arc without waiting for updates — a genuine luxury in this genre.

**Our score: 8.5/10** — For readers who like their overpowered protagonist with a healthy side of strategy.

[Read on MangaDex →](https://mangadex.org/title/6a468761-5bd6-4de0-a0cb-47cb456ac2e0)

### 6. Normal Duke

The title is the joke: there is nothing normal about this duke. This completed WEBTOON comedy ran for exactly 100 episodes, wrapping up its finale in August 2026 to strong reader engagement. The series commits fully to the comedic contrast between its protagonist's mundane self-perception and the chaos his presence causes wherever he goes — the 'ordinary person who is absurdly extraordinary' formula executed with consistent craft.

**Why it's great:** Completed series are rare in the WEBTOON comedy space, and the fact that this one stuck the landing — with finale episodes drawing consistent high engagement — signals a series that knew where it was going from the start. Clean beginning, middle, and end for readers who hate waiting.

**Our score: 8.4/10** — A complete, satisfying OP comedy that respects your time.

[Read on Webtoon →](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=Normal+Duke)

### 7. The Overpowered Newbie

New to the game, new to the world, absolutely not new to destroying everything in his path. This Tapas manhwa leans hard into the gap between beginner status and godlike capability for maximum comedic effect. The strong view counts per episode from launch indicate solid early reader retention — a good sign for a series built on first-impression comedy that needs to hook readers fast.

**Why it's great:** The 'newbie' framing keeps each new encounter feeling fresh. There's always something new to be hilariously overpowered against, and the format naturally creates comedy from the mismatch between the protagonist's inexperience and his results. It's comfort reading with consistent payoffs.

**Our score: 8.3/10** — Reliable comedic formula that doesn't overstay its welcome.

[Read on Tapas →](https://tapas.io/series/the-overpowered-newbie/info)

### 8. Mr. Delivery Knight

A delivery worker obsessed with a romantasy novel wakes up inside it — not as the hero, not as a chosen one, but still somehow the most dangerous person in the room. This short, completed WEBTOON comedy proves you don't need a long run to make a lasting impression. The premise of a delivery person navigating elaborate high-fantasy drama while completely outclassing every character he meets is executed with brisk, confident comedy that doesn't waste a single panel.

**Why it's great:** Two million views across three episodes is a remarkable ratio that speaks for itself. If you want a fast, genuinely funny read that doesn't demand any long-term commitment, this is the one to pick up on a lunch break.

**Our score: 8.2/10** — Maximum comedy per page; minimum time investment.
