## When the Hero Becomes the Monster

Not every manhwa protagonist plays by the rules. Some start as ordinary people and slowly embrace a darkness that reshapes them entirely. Others wake up in a villain's body and choose to own it. A few simply decide the world's rules no longer apply to them — and act accordingly. This list gathers the best manhwa where the MC turns evil, goes full antihero, or plants themselves firmly on the wrong side of the moral compass.

Whether you're drawn to cold revenge arcs, demonic cultivation, or villainess power fantasies, there's something here for every reader ready to root for the "bad" guy.

## Best Manhwa Where MC Turns Evil — Ranked

### 1. The Skeleton Soldier Failed to Defend the Dungeon

The Skeleton Soldier begins as a loyal, humble fighter sworn to protect Lady Succubus. When they're both brutally murdered, the skeleton is thrown back in time and forced to relive the nightmare again and again. With each loop, the gentle protector grows colder, more calculating, and more willing to cross lines it once would never have approached.

**Why it's great:** The gradual erosion of the skeleton's innocence is one of manhwa's most compelling character arcs. Watching loyalty curdle into something darker — without ever fully extinguishing that kernel of devotion — makes every chapter hit harder than the last.

**Our score: 9.1/10** — [Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/d993f789-e7e5-4832-92fd-37614220b427)

### 2. SSS-Class Revival Hunter

Gongja Kim was just another low-ranked hunter envying the stars of the Tower until a legendary copy skill changed everything. After being killed by the top hunter, Gongja inherits that hunter's abilities and is sent back in time. What follows is a cold, methodical revenge campaign that gradually transforms Gongja from an underdog into something far more dangerous.

**Why it's great:** The revenge fantasy is executed with rare psychological depth. Gongja doesn't just want to win — he wants his killer to feel every ounce of the helplessness he once endured. That obsession quietly remakes him into the very thing he once feared.

**Our score: 9.2/10** — [Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/4a973243-952e-44d7-a50f-883b4b7c9cc2)

### 3. Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint

Dokja Kim is the sole reader of a web novel that suddenly becomes reality. Armed with knowledge of every plot twist, he navigates an apocalyptic world with a calm that unsettles both allies and enemies alike. The further the story progresses, the more Dokja is willing to sacrifice — including pieces of himself — to reach the ending he wants.

**Why it's great:** Dokja's moral compass doesn't break — it bends deliberately. His choices grow increasingly dark, but always with a cold logic that's hard to argue against. It's a masterclass in writing a protagonist you root for even when you probably shouldn't.

**Our score: 9.3/10** — [Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/9a414441-bbad-43f1-a3a7-dc262ca790a3)

### 4. Second Life Ranker

Yeonwoo learns his twin brother died inside a deadly interdimensional Tower after being betrayed by those he trusted. Inheriting his brother's pocket watch and hidden legacy, Yeonwoo enters the Tower with one purpose: vengeance. He climbs relentlessly, accumulating dark powers and making choices no conventional hero would ever consider.

**Why it's great:** The grief underneath Yeonwoo's ruthlessness keeps him human even as his methods become brutal. Watching him absorb the powers of gods and demons while never quite losing his brother's memory makes this one of the most emotionally grounded evil-MC stories in the genre.

**Our score: 8.9/10** — [Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/1ffca916-3ad7-46d2-9591-a9b39e639971)

### 5. Descent of the Demonic Master

Jinho Kang carries a darkness that sets him apart from every other protagonist in the genre. After a life-altering trauma, he acquires demonic techniques that reshape not just his power but his entire worldview. Back in the modern world, he operates with a calm ruthlessness that makes allies wary and enemies regret crossing him.

**Why it's great:** Game Rant notes that Jinho "embodies a different kind of evil" — methodical rather than explosive, strategic rather than reactive. The contrast between his ordinary modern surroundings and his demonic inner nature creates sustained, fascinating tension.

**Our score: 9.0/10** — [Search on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=Descent+of+the+Demonic+Master)

### 6. Solo Leveling

Sung Jin-Woo starts as the weakest hunter alive — someone other hunters bring along out of pity. After a near-death encounter inside a double dungeon, he awakens a system that lets him level up indefinitely. The transformation is profound: where once he was desperate and afraid, he becomes utterly calm in the face of any threat, his emotions increasingly unreadable.

**Why it's great:** Solo Leveling isn't a pure villain story, but Jin-Woo's evolution into something beyond human is one of manhwa's defining arcs. The colder and more powerful he becomes, the more readers debate whether he's still a hero — or just the apex predator in the room.

**Our score: 9.5/10** — [Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/32d76d19-8a05-4db0-9fc2-e0b0648fe9d0)

### 7. Reverse Villain

In a world where destined heroes always claim glory, one man has spent countless lives as a powerless bystander. Fed up with the cycle, he resolves to become the villain of the story and pursues that role with terrifying conviction. His goal isn't domination for its own sake — it's to finally matter on his own terms.

**Why it's great:** The premise flips the hero fantasy in the most satisfying way. Rather than accidental evil, this MC consciously and deliberately chooses the villain path — and the series takes that choice seriously rather than treating it as a joke.

**Our score: 8.7/10** — [Search on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=Reverse+Villain)

### 8. Return of the Mad Demon

A martial artist dies at the height of his infamy, branded as the Mad Demon — the most feared figure in the cultivation world. Given a second chance, he doesn't try to reform or redeem himself. He pursues his personal goals with the same blunt, unapologetic ruthlessness that earned him his terrifying reputation the first time around.

**Why it's great:** The "Mad Demon" label isn't ironic — this MC genuinely operates outside normal moral frameworks and doesn't pretend otherwise. It's one of the most refreshingly honest villain-protagonist stories around: you always know exactly who you're dealing with.

**Our score: 8.8/10** — [Search on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=Return+of+the+Mad+Demon)

### 9. The Villainess Turns the Hourglass

Aria lived in luxury after her mother's marriage into nobility — until her stepsister Mielle engineered her execution. When time reverses, Aria doesn't try to become a better person. She studies Mielle's scheming playbook, masters it, and turns every trap back on its maker with cold, surgical precision.

**Why it's great:** The satisfaction here comes from Aria's total refusal to take the high road. She is the villain, and she makes sure she's a smarter one than the girl who killed her. Every moment of payback is earned, deliberate, and deeply pleasurable to read.

**Our score: 8.8/10** — [Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/73bc69fa-9ba9-4533-a243-ebc11651339f)

### 10. The Patron of Villains

Currently ranking among Webtoon's most popular series, The Patron of Villains follows a protagonist who actively supports, funds, and empowers villains rather than heroes. The story interrogates what "evil" actually means inside a world built around rigid heroic narratives.

**Why it's great:** It approaches the evil-MC concept from an organizational angle. The MC isn't just committing dark acts — they're building a power structure around villainy, giving the series a strategic depth that most action manhwa never attempt.

**Our score: 8.9/10** — [Search on Webtoon](https://www.webtoons.com/en/search?keyword=The+Patron+of+Villains)

### 11. Villains Are Destined to Die

Penelope Eckart wakes up inside a reverse harem dating sim as the game's villainess — on maximum difficulty, where every route ends in her death. Rather than fighting the villain label, Penelope leans into it, using manipulation and survival instinct to carve out a future the game never intended for her.

**Why it's great:** Penelope isn't evil by choice — she's evil because the game's rules demand it, and she's smart enough to weaponize those rules. Her methodical, desperate intelligence makes her one of the most compelling antiheroines the genre has produced.

**Our score: 8.6/10** — [Read on MangaDex](https://mangadex.org/title/14569f2f-f66a-4c67-ac7f-a37823a0fa23)
